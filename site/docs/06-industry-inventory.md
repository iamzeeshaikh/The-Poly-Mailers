## The problem each page solves

### Clothing and apparel

* **Problem:** One range spans hugely different garment bulks, with high return rates on top.
* **Angle unique to this page:** Treats packaging as a two-way flow rather than an outbound-only decision.

### Ecommerce

* **Problem:** Unit price is the visible cost; the expensive ones are invisible until volume rises.
* **Angle unique to this page:** Total cost per parcel, including the operational costs a quotation never shows.

### Fashion boutiques

* **Problem:** Presentation standards of a large brand, without the volume to justify large runs.
* **Angle unique to this page:** Where to spend and where not to at low volume — the opposite of the ecommerce page.

### Beauty and cosmetics

* **Problem:** Both the product and its printed carton have to arrive unmarked.
* **Angle unique to this page:** Protecting secondary packaging, not just contents — a requirement unique to this category.

### Books and stationery

* **Problem:** Paper damage is corners, creasing and moisture — not impact.
* **Angle unique to this page:** The only page on the site about protecting flat, rigid-but-bendable contents.

### Subscription businesses

* **Problem:** The same customer sees the packaging repeatedly, and the count is known in advance.
* **Angle unique to this page:** Repeat-arrival experience and forecastable ordering — problems no other category has.

### Accessories

* **Problem:** Items in one range vary too much in shape and fragility for a single specification.
* **Angle unique to this page:** How to cover a genuinely varied range with a small number of sizes.

### Small businesses

* **Problem:** Committing to artwork, sizes and quantities before any of them is settled.
* **Angle unique to this page:** A first-order sequence built around not committing, which no other page covers.

### Gift retail

* **Problem:** The recipient is not the buyer, and the parcel is the whole first impression.
* **Angle unique to this page:** The direct-to-recipient problem, which no other industry page addresses.

### Electronics accessories

* **Problem:** Hard-cornered retail cartons in a flexible bag, plus moisture and static considerations.
* **Angle unique to this page:** The static and moisture constraints, which appear in no other industry page.

## Sectors deliberately excluded

No page was written for categories that predominantly ship in cartons. A page
recommending a flexible bag for rigid, crush-sensitive goods would be a page
arguing against itself, and the industries index says so rather than leaving the
absence unexplained.
